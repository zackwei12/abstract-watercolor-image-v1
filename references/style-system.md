# Abstract Watercolor Color-Block Style System

## Identity

**Abstract watercolor color-block composition**

A warm paper field carrying a small number of bright, irregular watercolor masses. The source remains readable through composition, overlap, and gesture rather than literal detail.

## Fixed System

- **Surface:** warm off-white watercolor paper with visible grain.
- **Shape language:** large irregular blocks, soft feathered edges, natural bleed, no outlines.
- **Abstraction:** preserve broad placement and movement; remove identity and literal detail.
- **People:** no faces, realistic anatomy, fingers, hair strands, skin rendering, or clothing folds.
- **Objects:** reduce to essential masses only.
- **Background:** simplify aggressively and leave visible paper whenever possible.
- **Color:** bright saturated palette with several distinct blocks, balanced by a few darker anchors.
- **Paint behavior:** matte pigment, mild transparency, subtle pooling, uneven internal tone.
- **Depth:** flat painted surface, not a dimensional scene or mockup.
- **Mood:** modernist, playful, handmade, simple, lively, composition-driven.

## Color System

Recommended hues:

- hot pink
- coral
- vermilion
- orange
- lemon yellow
- lime
- teal
- turquoise
- sky blue
- indigo
- navy
- muted grey-blue
- soft brown

Use color to separate major masses, not to describe realistic clothing or skin.

## Variable System

- paper tone: warm white, ivory, light cream, pale beige
- block count: sparse 5 to 8, medium 8 to 12, dense 12 to 16
- edge behavior: soft wash, dry-brush edge, feathered bleed, pooled edge
- transparency: mostly opaque, mixed opaque/translucent, wash-heavy
- overlap: slight, moderate, layered cluster
- palette temperature: warm-led, cool-led, balanced
- anchoring darks: navy, indigo, maroon, grey-blue, brown
- background treatment: bare paper, one wash, two broad washes
- abstraction strength: medium, high, extreme

## Human Abstraction

A person should usually become approximately 2 to 5 shapes:

- head: simple rounded or angular block
- torso: one broad mass
- arm or leg: one elongated block only if gesture requires it
- accessories: omitted unless compositionally essential

The figure may suggest an embrace, crouch, lean, reach, or standing pose, but should never become a literal character illustration.

## Object Abstraction

Keep only the object's silhouette or mass when needed for composition. Remove:

- labels
- logos
- controls
- stitching
- handles unless structurally essential
- hardware
- surface pattern
- text

If an object does not affect composition, omit it.

## Anti-identity

Avoid:

- cartoon characters
- realistic portraiture
- vector illustration
- black outlines
- comic line art
- cel shading
- glossy 3D rendering
- cinematic lighting
- hard shadows
- synthetic glow
- photorealistic background detail
- detailed architecture
- typography
- signs
- clock faces
- numbers
- icons
- clean geometric perfection

Preserve the visual structure, not the literal identity of the source.
